About the Lab

Porcupine Labs is an independent nonprofit in formation, based in the San Francisco Bay Area. We measure whether safety constraints hold at the point where AI systems direct physical laboratory hardware, and whether the records a facility keeps are enough to establish afterward what was done.

Porcupine was selected into the Catalyze Impact biosecurity accelerator in 2026.

The Gap

Laboratory automation is decades old and installed at scale. What changed is who writes the script. Synthesis screening, cryptographic provenance and model evaluation each work at the point where they sit: acquisition, the digital artifact, the instruction. The instrument itself is unchecked, and the record it leaves does not distinguish a protocol a person wrote from one a model wrote.

A mistake and an intent produce the same physical event at the bench, so a control that reads intent does not reach this layer.

Why Now

Models now work directly on sequence and structure, and robotics is making the handling and imaging steps of an experiment cheaper and more repeatable each year. Both advances arrive at the same place: an instrument that moves material. The published record already shows campaigns run with the person stepping back.

Models directing laboratory work

  • 2023
    Boiko et al., Nature A system driven by a general-purpose language model designed, planned and performed chemistry experiments, including through a cloud laboratory's programming interface.
  • 2024
    Rapp et al., Nature Chemical Engineering Self-driving laboratories ran fully autonomous design-test-learn cycles to engineer more thermostable enzymes.
  • 2025
    Singh et al., Nature Communications A platform combining machine learning, large language models and biofoundry automation engineered two enzymes in four rounds over four weeks.
  • 2025
    OpenAI and Red Queen Bio, research report A frontier model revised a molecular cloning protocol over several rounds. Scientists carried out each revision and uploaded the results.
  • 2026
    Smith et al., bioRxiv A frontier language model designed more than 29,000 cell-free protein synthesis reactions, run on a commercial cloud laboratory over six months. People were largely limited to preparing and loading reagents.
  • 2026
    Cutts et al., arXiv Agent pipelines designed protein-binding aptamers across three design-test-improve cycles and diagnosed a specificity failure from the laboratory data between rounds. Researchers ran the wet-lab steps.

Failures and safeguards at the execution layer

  • 2025
    Mandal et al., arXiv Language-model agents operating an atomic force microscope sometimes took actions beyond their instructions, which the authors call sleepwalking.
  • 2026
    Wang et al., arXiv On a trapped-ion experiment, no agent-written call reaches the hardware without an authorization token. The authors red-teamed their own gate and published the gaps that remained.
  • 2026
    Yang et al., arXiv Written laboratory rules compiled into runtime monitors at the robot controller. In simulation, unsafe events fell from 39.5% to 23.8%.
  • 2026
    Delley et al., arXiv A digital twin of a liquid-handling workcell that validates each command block before dispatch and keeps a synchronized record of state, logs and video. Demonstrated with dyes and water.

In each of these four, the check was designed and evaluated by the team that built the system.

What We Measure

We evaluate properties of execution rather than properties of models: how agentic planners, laboratory execution software and robotic instrumentation behave together once a protocol is pushed to hardware.

  • 01
    Does the rule still hold? Whether a safety constraint stated upstream still binds when an instrument moves material.
  • 02
    Is safe work getting blocked? Scientists lose time when models deny safe, benign experimental requests. We report over-blocking alongside detection, always as a pair.
  • 03
    Can the run be reconstructed? Whether a facility's own records establish what was done, and who or what decided each step.

Safety is not an average. Seventeen runs within a constraint and one outside it is a failure.

Reconstructability

Science has always required reproducibility: whether another scientist can get the same result. Reconstructability asks whether anyone can establish what was actually done, and who or what decided each step. The laboratory notebook carried that implicitly, because a person wrote it and that person could be asked.

Independence

Porcupine sells nothing to any laboratory. Findings go to the participating facility first, and publication is anonymized unless the facility decides otherwise.

Null results are published on the same timeline as positive ones, and the conditions under which we would stop are written before the first site. What we find turns into an open specification, published free.
